# ADR 0015: Relicense from Apache 2.0 to AGPL-3.0

Status: Accepted

## Context

Boxy was licensed under Apache 2.0 since the project's `LICENSE` file was
added — permissive, permitting essentially unrestricted use, including a
third party taking the project and building a closed-source commercial
product or service on top of it without contributing anything back or even
disclosing what they built. #212 flagged that this wasn't clearly the
intended posture, without committing to a specific replacement.

The concern, stated directly by the maintainer: personal use, internal
company use, modification, and contribution should all remain unrestricted.
What should not be permitted is someone taking Boxy, building a closed-source
commercial product or service on top of it, and giving nothing back.

Several license families were compared against that specific shape:

- **PolyForm Noncommercial** — blocks *all* commercial use, including the
  internal-company-use case the maintainer explicitly wants to keep open.
  Rejected as too broad.
- **BUSL/FSL** (time-delayed conversion to a permissive license after a
  future date) — solves a different problem: protecting a vendor from a
  well-funded competitor undercutting them for a fixed window, not a
  reciprocity/give-back requirement. Rejected as the wrong shape for the
  stated concern.
- **Elastic License 2.0** — narrower than the concern: blocks reselling the
  software itself as a managed/hosted service, but does not require sharing
  back modifications used for other closed-source purposes (e.g. embedding
  Boxy's code into a closed-source internal tool that isn't literally "Boxy
  as a service"). Rejected as underinclusive.
- **AGPL-3.0** — permits any use, including commercial and internal, but
  requires that anyone who modifies the software and lets users interact
  with the modified version over a network (not limited to public-facing
  SaaS — this includes a company's own internal network) make that modified
  source available to those users. This is the license family actually
  designed to close the "run it as a network service without distributing
  source" gap that plain GPL leaves open, and it matches the stated concern
  directly rather than approximately.

Known critiques of AGPL-3.0 were weighed against Boxy's actual situation,
not treated as automatically disqualifying:

- **Blanket corporate bans.** Google's own published policy
  (<https://opensource.google/documentation/reference/using/agpl-policy>)
  and many other large companies ban AGPL dependencies outright rather than
  reviewing case by case, because the network-interaction trigger is broad.
  This cuts against "internal company use should be fine" in practice at
  companies with such a blanket policy. Accepted as a real but currently
  low-cost tradeoff: Boxy is not yet seeking wide corporate adoption as a
  dependency (`CONTRIBUTING.md` isn't accepting outside contributions yet
  either), so this cost is mostly deferred, not eliminated.
- **Doesn't stop a well-resourced actor from complying on paper while still
  capturing most of the value** (publish the modified source, keep the
  commercial advantage) — this is why MongoDB/Elastic/HashiCorp moved to
  SSPL/ELv2/BSL instead of relying on AGPL. Accepted: that failure mode
  specifically matters to vendors defending a commercial moat against a
  cloud-provider clone of their own product; it is not the maintainer's
  stated concern (reciprocity, not competitive protection).
- **Enforcement burden falls on the copyright holder** with no automatic
  teeth. Accepted as an inherent property of any copyleft choice, not
  specific to AGPL over the alternatives considered.
- **Contribution-chilling effect** on projects courting outside
  contributors. Accepted as currently low-cost since Boxy isn't accepting
  outside contributions yet; revisit if that changes.
- **"Internal use" is not actually obligation-free under AGPL** — the
  network-interaction trigger applies to a company's own employees using a
  modified internal deployment, not only public users. This is a real
  narrowing of what "internal company use is fine" means in practice
  (internal use is fine *unmodified*; a modified internal deployment still
  owes source to the employees interacting with it) — accepted as
  consistent with, not contrary to, the stated intent of requiring give-back
  for anything built on top of Boxy.

## Decision

Relicense Boxy from Apache 2.0 to **AGPL-3.0**, effective 2026-08-28.

- `LICENSE` now contains the verbatim, unmodified canonical AGPL-3.0 text
  (sourced from <https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.txt>), matching how
  the prior Apache 2.0 `LICENSE` file was also left as unmodified boilerplate
  rather than hand-edited with a filled-in copyright notice.
- The copyright/attribution notice and a plain-language summary of what
  AGPL-3.0 actually requires live in README's `## License` section instead
  of being hand-inserted into the canonical license text.
- No SPDX license identifiers exist anywhere in the source tree today (none
  were added under Apache 2.0 either), so none were introduced as part of
  this change. Revisit as a separate decision if per-file headers are wanted
  later.

**Why now, not later:** `git log --all --format='%an <%ae>'` confirmed zero
external human contributors as of this decision — only the maintainer's own
account (two casing variants), bot commits (Copilot, dependabot,
github-actions), and AI-authored commits under this project's existing
co-author convention. Relicensing today is a unilateral edit; once outside
contributions are accepted (see `CONTRIBUTING.md`), relicensing would need
every contributor's consent, which is materially harder to coordinate
retroactively.

## Non-goals / explicitly out of scope

- **Per-file SPDX headers.** Not part of this change; the repo has never
  used them, under either license.
- **A NOTICE file.** Apache 2.0's `NOTICE`-file convention doesn't carry
  over to AGPL-3.0, and none existed previously.
- **Revisiting this decision on a schedule.** This is not a BUSL-style
  time-delayed license; there is no planned future conversion back to a
  permissive license baked into this decision.

## Consequences

- Anyone consuming Boxy as a dependency, or a company evaluating internal
  adoption, now needs to be aware of AGPL-3.0's network-interaction
  disclosure trigger — including for internal-only modified deployments, not
  just public SaaS. This is a real adoption-friction cost, accepted
  knowingly per the Context section above.
- Prior Apache-2.0-licensed releases (all tags/releases up to and including
  whatever was last published before this ADR) remain available to their
  recipients under Apache 2.0 — relicensing is not retroactive. Only source
  going forward from this change is AGPL-3.0.
- If Boxy later opens up to outside contributions, contributor license
  expectations (CLA, DCO, or implicit inbound=outbound under AGPL-3.0) need
  a separate, explicit decision before accepting external PRs — not
  addressed by this ADR.
